# EXP-001 conditional forecasting protocol

**Status: frozen conditional on GO.** The [data audit](DATA_AUDIT.md) found retrospective overlap and issued NO-GO for this historical experiment. Activate this protocol only after documenting the boundaries and dated feature releases. This issue ends at the data audit and protocol.

## Hypothesis and forecast clock

For comparable U.S. metro areas, past Realtor.com relative listing attention reduces out-of-sample error in forecasts of Zillow raw monthly newly pending listings after accounting for historical pending activity, seasonality and aligned controls. The null is no improvement over the matched baseline. One month is the primary horizon. Three months is secondary and forecasts the third month's count. The comparison tests incremental predictive information about activity on two platforms. It cannot estimate a purchase conversion rate, willingness to pay or the effect of changing attention.

For calendar month `t`, set the forecast origin at the end of month `t` in U.S. Eastern time. Predict the full calendar months `t+1` and `t+3`. The latest admissible housing observation month is `t-1` and all feature releases must also have been available by the origin. For example, an origin at the end of October uses September housing observations only if their dated releases were available by October 31. It predicts November and January. If any required `t-1` value is unavailable, omit that metro-origin from both models. Preserve timestamps and historical files so availability can be audited.

## Data eligibility and controls

- Require a dated, one-to-one Zillow RegionID to CBSA bridge and verified matching county composition for the source periods and release vintages used. Exclude ambiguous or changing geographies unless both sources are verified on a common stable footprint at each origin. A county match against the legacy map alone is insufficient.
- Before fitting, document the sampling frame, source files and release timestamps, hashes, measured complete-case counts by split and each exclusion. Use housing vintages available at each origin, including quality flags available then. Lock a primary control set using the training availability audit alone. Include raw Zillow inventory and median list price only if their geography and timing can be verified, and Freddie Mac's PMMS only if release vintages can be verified. A control that fails verification is omitted from both models for every split. Record the retained set before tuning.
- Use `t-1` observations for included controls. Compute the national rate as the arithmetic mean of all published weekly observations dated in `t-1`, using the values known at the origin and the actual observation dates, including holiday releases. An incomplete month is missing. Document the November 2022 PMMS break and report a predefined sensitivity omitting the rate from both models.
- Use exact month lags and leave missing or flagged features missing. Do not interpolate, carry values across gaps or convert missing counts to zeros. Forecast pairs must share metros, origins, targets and the control availability mask. Record exclusions by month, metro and reason. Require at least 30 eligible metros and at least 12 scored origin months per horizon in each validation and test window, with at least 12 training target months both before 2022 and during 2022–23. Use only metros represented in training. Coverage is a feasibility gate, with uncertainty assessed at the time-block level.
- For each target, score against the first published raw monthly Zillow count for its verified geography. Missing first-release outcomes are unscored in both models and counted as exclusions. At every fitting cutoff, a supervised example is eligible only after its target release. Preserve each feature row as it appeared at its original forecast cutoff. If housing histories cannot be reconstructed, retain NO-GO for this historical test. A fixed-vintage analysis would answer a retrospective question and requires a separately specified design.

## Chronological splits

Use the verified subset of the audited 2018-01–2026-09 span. Windows are indexed by **target month** and applied identically across metros. Twelve-month features may make the first eligible target later than the first available observation.

| Stage | Target months | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initial training | 2018-01–2023-12 | Fit on pre-shift history through 2021 and the 2022–23 regime. |
| Validation | 2024-01–2025-03 | Rolling forecast origins to select regularization and assess incremental signal. |
| Later test | 2025-04–2026-09 | Held-out forecasts from the later period with no tuning on these outcomes. |

At each validation and test origin, use an expanding fit with only rows whose labels were released by that origin. Enforce the split boundary for tuning. Freeze choices for each horizon using validation scores whose labels were already available at the **first** test origin for that horizon. Under a mid-next-month Zillow schedule this implies validation labels through February 2025 for one month and December 2024 for three months. Score the remaining validation forecasts as diagnostics without feeding them into selection. Test-period refits can use newly available labels according to the frozen schedule and hyperparameters. Fit and score in chronological order. Never use a later test-origin outcome to change the specification or selection. Earlier-than-planned starts, a shorter window or a changed target vintage require a new protocol before looking at test scores.

Report the 2022–23 regime separately in a diagnostic rolling replay wherever historical training and release availability permit. Compare this to the later validation and test periods. The diagnosis describes error variation over time. The PMMS measurement change and platform revisions may coincide with market changes and cannot be disentangled by the calendar split.

## Baselines and conditional extension

1. **Seasonal naive reference.** Use the count in the same target calendar month one year earlier as it was known at the origin. Report on the common support of all reported models.
2. **Regularized autoregression with controls.** Fit a pooled ridge regression separately for each horizon on `log1p(pending[t+h])`. Include an intercept, metro indicators, target month-of-year indicators, `log1p` pending at `t-1`, `t-2`, `t-3` and `t-12`, and `t-1` controls. Use `log1p` for inventory and price and keep rate in percentage points before standardization. Standardize continuous features on each available training fold and use a unit scale for zero variance. Use an unpenalized intercept and minimize mean squared training loss plus a ridge penalty on other coefficients. Choose the penalty from `{0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}` by validation MAE in count units, breaking ties toward stronger regularization. Back-transform with `expm1` and clip negative predictions to zero.
3. **Attention-augmented ridge.** Add only `log1p(relative attention)` at `t-1`, `t-2` and `t-3`. Fit on the same rows and use the same model class, search grid, fold schedule and scoring. Use the same matched sample for validation and test.
4. **Conditional latent sequential model.** Consider a pooled one-dimensional linear Gaussian state-space model only after the ridge comparison meets the stopping rule below. Let a scalar metro state follow an AR(1) with shared parameters and enter the log pending observation equation. Use lagged controls and seasonal terms in that equation and log attention as a separate noisy observation of the state. Compare with an otherwise matched state-space model with the attention observation removed, and with the augmented ridge. Use filtering through the forecast cutoff and propagate the state and its uncertainty to each horizon. A state inferred with future observations cannot enter a forecast. Estimate parameters on training data and use a fresh untouched evaluation window for the extension because the original test informs the decision to extend. The state summarizes predictive variation in the measured series.

Build each horizon directly from origin-time information. Use trailing windows only if specified before fitting, with every constituent released by the origin. No centered smoothing, whole-panel scaling, revised historical controls, contemporaneous pending labels, or realized intermediate features for three-month forecasts. A later revision can enter an expanding fit only according to the declared vintage rules.

## Evaluation and stopping rule

- **Primary score:** For each target month compute MAE in listing counts over the matched metros, then average monthly MAE with equal month weights. Report the augmented-versus-baseline paired loss difference and percent improvement separately by horizon. Report count RMSE, median metro MAE and the distribution of metro-level differences as diagnostics. Include scored counts by target month and metro.
- **Uncertainty:** Use a paired circular moving-block bootstrap over target months, sampling whole cross-sections in six-month blocks with 5,000 replicates and a fixed seed of 1. Report 95% percentile intervals. Use three-month blocks as a sensitivity. Common shocks and overlapping three-month horizons make independent metro-month intervals inappropriate. With an 18-month test window, intervals are descriptive and may be unstable.
- **Probabilistic evaluation:** For ridge distributions, use the empirical distribution of signed log-scale forecast errors from eligible rolling forecasts made before the origin under the same configuration, with at least 12 completed forecast months. Add residual draws to the point forecast in log space and back-transform and clip. When a predictive distribution is available for both models, report CRPS in count units and empirical 50%, 80% and 95% interval coverage and widths on matched cases. Report the number of calibration-eligible origins. For the latent model use the filtered forecast distribution.
- **Decision:** Stop if the data gate fails. After GO, stop at the simple comparisons unless one-month validation MAE improves by at least 2%, the frozen one-month test MAE also improves by at least 2%, and the paired 95% test interval for augmented minus baseline MAE lies below zero. The 2% threshold is a predeclared practical minimum. Treat three-month findings as secondary. If the gate fails, report the effect sizes and uncertainty and end the experiment. If it passes, the extension becomes eligible for separate work with a fresh evaluation window. No model fitting or extension work belongs to Issue #1.
